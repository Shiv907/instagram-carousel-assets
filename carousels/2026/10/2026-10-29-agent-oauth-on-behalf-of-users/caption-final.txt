Giving your agent an admin token is the fastest way to build it, and the worst way to ship it. It should act with your user's access, nothing more 👇

There are two halves. Inbound auth decides who can call the agent. Outbound auth decides what the agent can reach on that user's behalf.

The user signs in and approves one provider, say GitHub. The token is stored for that user only, which is called session binding.

From then on, every tool call runs with that user's permissions and nothing more.

Use the smallest scopes that work, and give users a way to revoke access. And never paste tokens into prompts or logs.

AWS added a managed Consent portal to AgentCore Identity for exactly this, aimed at agents used from Kiro, Claude Code, Cursor and VS Code. Tokens land in the AgentCore Identity token vault.
