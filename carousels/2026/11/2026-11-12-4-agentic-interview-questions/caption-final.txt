4 questions that trip agent engineers, and none of them is "explain this framework".

What I see in agent interviews is less about recall and more about judgment. What do you do when a tool fails? When does a human have to approve a write? What happens when your tool list gets huge? And how do you actually pick a decoding strategy?

I picked 4 questions word for word from a public GitHub list of company-wise questions. The company tags on that list are unverified, so don't read them as confirmed. For each one, the slides give our model answers: what the interviewer is really checking, plus a strong outline.

The questions:
1) How do you handle tool-call errors, timeouts and retries in an agentic loop?
2) Design human-in-the-loop approval for an agent that takes consequential actions.
3) How many tools is too many? How do you design tool schemas an LLM can actually use correctly?
4) Compare greedy, beam search, top-k, top-p and temperature sampling. When does each fail?

Honestly, number 1 is where most people give the "just retry three times" answer.

Which one would trip you up? Tell me in the comments, and save this before your next agent interview.
