Your LLM judge doesn't need to read every single turn. Let a calibrated model screen them first 👇

Here's the idea. A decision model is a small, specialized model that picks from a fixed set of answers and gives you a calibrated probability, not free-form text. It handles three question types: yes/no (noul), choice (up to 255 options) and score (2 to 10 ordered levels).

In AgentCore Evaluations it plugs in as a code-based evaluator. A Lambda gets the agent's OpenTelemetry spans, calls the model, and returns a label and a value.

The part I like is the cascade. Screen every turn with the decision model, and only send the turns below your threshold to the built-in LLM evaluators for an explanation. An LLM judge gives you a score and an explanation in 2 to 10 seconds. A decision model gives you a calibrated probability in hundreds of milliseconds.

One warning before you gate a release on it. Validate on your own traffic first: 3 human labelers, majority vote, then check agreement, calibration and where they disagree. And pick a threshold per evaluator instead of just defaulting to 0.5.

The ECE numbers on slide 7 are illustrative, so you can check the maths yourself.
