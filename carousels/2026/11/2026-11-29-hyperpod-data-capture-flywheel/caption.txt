Your eval set is frozen in the past. Your production traffic isn't.

AWS published a full walkthrough of this loop on SageMaker HyperPod, and I like how unglamorous it is. A support-ticket router picks one of 6 labels. The retailer launches a new line of air conditioners. The router has no label for them, so it quietly sends them to "Other". Offline benchmarks look fine. Only the captured traffic shows the problem.

The loop, on one cluster:

1. Capture: HyperPod Data Capture writes every request and response to S3 (Tier 3, model pod)
2. Analyze: Athena queries the files in place. "Other" went from 4.8% of tickets before the launch to 36.8% after
3. Label: a SQL CASE policy relabels the records. Mixed signals go to human review
4. Fine-tune: a LoRA run with the HyperPod training operator on a second GPU (60 seconds in their run)
5. Redeploy: side by side, with capture still on

Result on 280 held-out tickets: 235 correct before, 280 after. The new class went from 0/40 to 40/40, with no drop on the 5 old classes. In AWS's latency test, the capture proxy added no measurable latency.

The honest part: it's synthetic data, and on 24 hand-written probes the new model scored 20/24. It learned shortcuts like "cooling means AC". Which is exactly why capture stays on for round two.

My take: the SQL labeling policy is the real idea here. Your Athena finding becomes a reviewable, re-runnable rule instead of a pile of hand labels.
