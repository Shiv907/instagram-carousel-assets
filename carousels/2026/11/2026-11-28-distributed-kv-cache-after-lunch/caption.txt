Your agent took minutes to reply after lunch. The model didn't get slower. The GPU had to re-read the whole conversation.

Here's what's going on. Every request has two phases. Prefill reads the whole prompt and computes key/value (KV) tensors for every token. Decode then writes one token at a time and reuses that KV cache. Time to first token is mostly prefill.

That KV cache lives in GPU memory (and host RAM or NVMe) on one server. Come back after a break, land on a different server, or lose a node, and it's gone. So the GPU recomputes KV for the system prompt, the tools and every earlier message before it writes a word.

AWS's fix (GA on 18 Nov): a shared L2 KV cache on Amazon ElastiCache for Valkey, in the same AZ as your GPU fleet, read over RDMA on EFA. vLLM, SGLang and TensorRT-LLM plug in through the open-source LMCache connector.

AWS's own claims: up to 40% lower cost per million tokens and up to 80% lower TTFT. Your number depends on your hit rate, so measure first.

That's what the new distributed KV caching skill in the Agent Toolkit for AWS is for. It runs inside your coding agent, reads your fleet read-only, estimates savings from your own traffic with KVTrace, and asks before it creates anything.

My take: if your prompts are short or never repeat, skip it. If your agents carry long histories across many GPU servers, run KVTrace before you buy more GPUs.
