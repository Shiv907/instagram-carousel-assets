Your agent passed CI. It's still not production-ready.

AWS published a measured example that shows why, and it stuck with me. A policy assistant answered 3 HR questions over a knowledge base. Chunk-level precision was 1.00 on all three. A release gate built on that metric would have shipped every answer.

Then look at fact-level precision: 0.59, 0.53 and 0.09. On the sick-leave question, about nine in ten retrieved facts were irrelevant, and retrieval coverage was 0.00. The corpus simply had no dedicated sick-leave policy.

Next they changed only the system prompt. One answer got better. The sick-leave one got worse. No prompt can fix missing source material.

Here's what I'm taking from the Agent DLC idea:

1. Agents are non-deterministic. Run each example k times and judge the pass rate, not one assertion.
2. Prompts, tools, retrieval and memory settings are source code. Version them, with an evaluation attached.
3. Dependencies move on their own, so re-run your evals on a schedule.
4. Split your criteria into hard constraints, release gates and monitoring-only.
5. Change one thing at a time, then re-evaluate.

One caveat: AWS labels this a reference implementation, not a benchmark or a customer result.
